About 2D1

2D1 is an independent design practice by Kosuke Futsukaichi. A place to work with clients, make things of my own, and share everyday objects I call Familiar Characters.

Those interests take the shape of a studio and a shop. Both come from an appreciation for design with a clear point of view and a habit of being particular about the things around me.

About me

Born in Japan, made in Arizona. That’s what I usually tell people to give them a sense of me. It says something about my blood and culture with some humor baked in.

This is how I approach my work: a clear idea, expressed simply, with room for humor. I call it Reduction with Wit. Whether I’m working on an identity, an interface, or an object, I try to edit down the nonessentials while keeping what gives it character.

My design education is rooted in the teaching of my professor Russell Taylor at Simon Fraser University, and the italiaDesign field school he ran. He gave me a foundation in design history, methods, and philosophy that helped me develop my own point of view. The way Russell and the Italian designers talked about design helped me see it more as an attitude, rather than something you do.

After graduating, I began my career working at multiple startups before joining Takram, a design agency in Tokyo. Moving between experience, product, and brand design has given me different ways to approach a problem, and more ideas for things I hope to make.

Studio and Shop

2D1 gives those interests a place to meet. In the studio, I work with founders and small businesses on brands, websites, and digital products. In the shop, I develop my own objects and tools.

I like client work. I respect people who start their own thing, and I enjoy helping them make something they believe in. I also want to follow my own ideas, including the silly, persistent ones, like wanting to design a shoehorn.

The two sides feed each other. Client work brings me into worlds I wouldn’t enter on my own. My own projects give me room to experiment and learn by making. What I learn in one finds its way into the other.

Familiar Characters

Alongside making things, I collect and share objects I find in the wild, calling them Familiar Characters. Finding similarities among the things I gravitate toward helps me understand what matters to me, and what I hope to bring to my own work.

I’m fascinated by how an ordinary thing can become important to someone: we have our go-to mug, a pen that feels right in our hand, a shoehorn used every time before heading out the door. Something caught our attention at first: a proportion, a color, a detail that tickled our senses. We keep reaching for it because it works well and feels right in our lives. Over time, it gathers marks, habits, and memories.

What starts as an object with character becomes a character in our lives. That’s what I mean by Familiar Characters. Objects become meaningful because life happens around them, and that’s what I hope the things I make will have the chance to do.
