2D1/KOSUKE FUTSUKAICHI/FAMILIAR CHARACTERS

2D1 is an independent design practice by Kosuke Futsukaichi. My focus is digital product design and branding. Alongside client work, I collect things I want and make things I wish existed. I share the collection as Familiar Characters.

These interests take shape as a studio and a shop. In the studio, I work with founders and small businesses on brands, websites, and digital products. The shop is home to my own products. I gravitate toward things that are practical, fun, and opinionated.

The two rooms feed each other. Client work brings me into areas I wouldn’t enter on my own. My own projects give me room to experiment and learn by making. What I learn in one finds its way into the other.

I’m a Japanese-Canadian designer raised on North American culture. I approach my work with a clear idea, expressed simply, while leaving room for fun. I call it Reduction with Wit. Whether I’m working on an identity, an interface, or an object, I try to strip away the nonessentials while preserving what gives it character.

This approach was shaped by my professor Russell Taylor at Simon Fraser University and the italiaDesign field school he ran. His passion and attitude resonated with many people, including me. His teaching in design history, philosophy, and methods helped me develop my own point of view. Through Russell and the Italian designers, I came to see design as a way of thinking rather than just a set of skills.

After graduating, I worked at several startups in Canada and the U.S. before joining a design agency in Tokyo. Moving between experience, product, and brand design has given me different ways to approach a problem and iterate on a form.

Alongside making things, I curate and share objects under the name Familiar Characters. This collection has lived on my phone and computer for years, so I wanted to give it a proper place on the internet. Finding similarities among the things I like helps me understand what matters to me and what I want to bring to my work.

I’m fascinated by how an ordinary thing can become important to someone: a go-to mug, a pen that feels right in the hand, a shoehorn we use every time we head out the door. Something first caught our attention: a proportion, a color, a detail that tickled our senses. We keep reaching for it because it works well and feels right in our lives. Over time, it gathers marks and memories, becoming part of our routines.

What starts as an object with character becomes a character in our lives. That’s what I mean by Familiar Characters. Objects become meaningful because life happens around them. I hope the things I make become part of someone’s life, too.
