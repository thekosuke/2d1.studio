Why familiar things matter

On character, familiarity, and the everyday objects we choose to keep. Notes from a design studio with a shop of its own.

What is 2D1?

2D1 is an independent design studio with a shop of its own. For companies, we find a clear idea and give it character, from the first strategy to the products and brands people use every day. For everyone else, we make everyday objects under our own name, each one hoping to become someone’s favorite something.

Who is behind it?

Why I started it

Most of the things we own are forgettable. They do their job and then disappear into the background of our lives. But a few of them stay. A mug that has moved with us through three apartments. The pen we always reach for first. A bag that has taken the shape of everything we have carried in it. We don’t keep these things because they are rare or expensive. We keep them because they became familiar.

A familiar object is not the same as a boring one. The things we grow attached to almost always have something particular about them: a proportion that feels right in the hand, a detail that makes us smile, a way of doing their job that is a little better than it needed to be. That particular something is character. It is what makes us notice an object in the first place, and what makes us forgive its imperfections later.

Character alone isn’t enough, though. Plenty of objects are full of personality and still end up in a drawer. What turns character into attachment is time. An object has to be useful often enough, and easy enough to live with, that it becomes part of the routine. Familiarity gives it meaning.

Character is what draws us in, but familiarity is what gives them meaning. Through everyday use and the moments that happen around them, these objects accumulate stories, memories, and a sense of familiarity. Over time, what starts as an object with character becomes a character in our lives—something personal, meaningful, and worth keeping for a long time.

Objects become meaningful because life happens around them.

Why it should exist

This is how we work, for clients and for ourselves. We edit until the idea is clear and the object is easy to live with, then we keep the one detail that gives it a reason to be remembered. Reduction without wit is sterile; wit without reduction is noise. We look for the point where the two meet.

It is also why we care about ordinary things. A water bottle, a tote bag, a tray by the door: these are the objects that spend the most time with us, so they are the ones most worth getting right.

We think of everything we make as living in the same place, a house full of familiar characters. Some we design for other companies. Some we make under our own name. Some we simply admire and keep around. What connects them is not a style, a price, or a category, but a way of seeing: the belief that the best everyday objects go from having character to becoming a character in someone’s life.

That is what we are trying to make. Not things that demand attention, but things that earn a place. Things that, after a while, you would miss.
