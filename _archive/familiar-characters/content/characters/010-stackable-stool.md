Most furniture wants to be looked at. This stool mostly wants to be moved. It is light enough to carry with one hand, low enough to work as a side table, and it stacks, so four of them take up the floor space of one. The three legs are the clever part: a three-legged stool never wobbles, whatever the floor is doing, which matters more in an old house than any amount of styling.

The seat is a plain disc. Each leg meets it with a single bent joint, and the whole construction can be read from any angle; there is nothing to hide, so nothing is hidden. It has been in production, more or less unchanged, for decades. That is usually a sign that someone got the first version right.

We use ours as a seat, a plant stand, a bedside table, a step up to the top shelf, and a place to put a bag down by the door. It has been knocked over, rained on, and painted around. The finish has worn through where hands pick it up, which is the most honest record of use an object can keep, and it looks better for it.

Some objects become familiar because they were designed to be. This one became familiar because it was always there.
